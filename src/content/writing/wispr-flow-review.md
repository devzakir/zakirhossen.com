I build software products alone. A large part of my working day is spent
giving instructions to coding agents, mainly Claude Code. Since January 2025 I have
given a large share of those instructions by voice, with Wispr Flow.

This is not a review written after a week of testing. The app keeps a local
database of every dictation, so I read it. Every number below comes from that
database on my Mac, read on 7 November 2026, or from my own receipts and support
emails.

## The short answer

Wispr Flow works, and I still use it. I paid for Pro for one month, in
April 2026. Today I am on the free plan, and it covers what I do. If you write
long prompts to AI tools all day, try the free plan first.

## My numbers

| Since 9 January 2025 | |
|---|---|
| Dictations | 3,442 |
| Words dictated | 147,177 |
| Days I used it | 328 |
| Time spent speaking | about 20 hours |
| Speaking pace | 123 words a minute |
| Typical dictation | 13 seconds, 27 words |
| Time until the text appears | 1.1 seconds typical, 3.1 seconds or more for 1 in 10 |

The speaking pace is the reason I keep using it. At 40 words a minute, typing
147,000 words would take about 61 hours. I spoke them in about 20.

## Where the words go

| App | Words | Share |
|---|---|---|
| iTerm (Claude Code) | 84,308 | 57% |
| Brave | 19,119 | 13% |
| Claude desktop app | 11,281 | 8% |
| Chrome | 11,047 | 8% |
| Cursor | 8,622 | 6% |
| Apple Notes | 6,317 | 4% |
| WhatsApp | 839 | 1% |

More than half of everything I say goes into a terminal, to a coding agent. A
typical dictation of mine is 27 words. That is a full instruction with its
background, not a short command.

Speaking more does not mean checking less, though. Every change still goes
through the [review rules I use for AI-written code](/writing/claude-code-review/).

## What it costs, and what I paid

| Plan | Billed monthly | Billed yearly |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | $0 |
| Pro | $15 / user / month | $12 / user / month |
| Growth | $23 / user / month | $18 / user / month |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom |

Prices from the [Wispr Flow pricing page](https://wisprflow.ai/pricing), checked on
7 November 2026. The free plan has "generous weekly usage" and no card. The page
does not say how many words that is.

I paid $15 for one month of Pro, from 10 April to 10 May 2026. That was my
heaviest month: I dictated 42,561 words in it. Before paying, I asked
support for 40% off, and then 30%. The reply came from an AI support agent. It
said Wispr does not give custom discounts. It offered three things instead:

- yearly billing, which brings Pro to $12 a month ($144 a year)
- a free month of Pro for each friend who signs up and dictates 2,000 words
- 40% off for education, veterans, non-profits, accessibility and seniors, and
  50% off for students

Today the app shows the free plan. My biggest week since May was about 6,000
words, and nothing stopped me.

## What works

- **It types into every app.** The terminal, the browser, Cursor and WhatsApp
  all work the same way. I do not switch tools to dictate.
- **Bangla works.** Bangla is my first language. 123 of my dictations were
  detected as Bangla, and 103 of them came out in Bangla script.
- **The dictionary learns my words.** I have 21 entries: my product names like
  JuggleHire and ShipTell, my company name, and the names of people I work with.
- **Meeting notes reach my agents.** I have used the Notetaker since August 2026
  for 28 meetings, about 14 hours. Wispr has an MCP server, so Claude Code can
  read those notes directly, and a small script files each new meeting into my
  notes every hour.

## What goes wrong

- **Some dictations fail.** Of 3,442, 98 were dismissed, 31 came back empty,
  and 136 were pasted as a raw transcript without formatting. That is about 1 in
  13 that did not give me clean text.
- **It sometimes writes text I never said.** 19 dictations were tagged with a
  language I do not speak: Indonesian, Swahili, Korean, Portuguese and Hindi.
  Some were English with the wrong tag. Others were invented. One came out as a
  Korean TV news sign-off ("MBC News, I'm Lee Deok-young") when I had said
  nothing like it. Speech models can produce text like this when they get
  silence or noise. Read before you press Enter.
- **The local database grows.** On my Mac it is 574 MB. It also stores the audio
  of recent dictations: 146 recordings, 146 MB, from October and November. If
  you dictate private things, know that the audio is on your disk.
- **Some dictations are slow.** One in ten takes 3 seconds or more to appear.
  That is long enough to start typing again.

## Who should pay for it

Start on the free plan. Pay for Pro when you hit the weekly limit, or when you
need unlimited Notetaker history or a shared team dictionary. I paid in the
month I dictated 42,561 words. In my biggest week since then, about 6,000
words, the free plan was enough.
